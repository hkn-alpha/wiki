# NPRE321

NPRE321 (Introduction to Plasmas and Applications) is a 3 credit hour course offered in the fall. For ECE students, it is an elective option for the Semiconductor Engineering minor and introduces plasma concepts used in semiconductor fabrication, including deposition, etching, and material processing. The course also covers other plasma applications, including space propulsion and nuclear fusion.

## Content Covered

- Review of electricity and magnetism
- Plasma properties, gas discharges, and plasma generation
- Debye shielding, plasma frequency, and plasma sheaths
- Charged-particle motion in electric and magnetic fields
- Collisions, diffusion, and plasma transport
- Plasma-material interactions
- Plasma deposition, etching, and atmospheric plasmas
- Space plasmas and plasma thrusters
- Nuclear fusion and plasma confinement
- Plasma diagnostics

The course begins with an electromagnetism review, including Coulomb’s law, Gauss’s law, the Lorentz force, and Maxwell’s equations. Students then learn how to characterize a plasma through quantities such as density, temperature, Debye length, and plasma frequency. Gas discharges and the Paschen curve introduce how plasmas form and how breakdown depends on operating conditions.

The course then covers charged-particle motion in electric and magnetic fields. Topics include cyclotron frequency, Larmor radius, E×B drift, gradient-B drift, curvature drift, and polarization drift. Students also learn about plasma sheaths, including sheath potentials, the Bohm criterion, and the Child–Langmuir law. Collisions and transport are introduced through mean free path, collision frequency, mobility, diffusion, and plasma resistivity.

The applications portion is particularly relevant to ECE students interested in semiconductor manufacturing. It covers plasma-material interactions such as sputtering, implantation, and electron emission, followed by deposition and etching. Students are introduced to capacitively and inductively coupled plasmas, magnetron sources, and atmospheric plasmas. The course also discusses plasma chemistry and surface treatment.

Other applications include space plasmas, Hall and helicon thrusters, and nuclear fusion. The fusion unit covers confinement, fusion reactions, the Lawson criterion, breakeven and ignition, tokamaks, stellarators, and inertial fusion. Students also learn about electric and magnetic probes, spectroscopy, and other diagnostic methods used to study plasmas.

## Prerequisites

The official prerequisites listed in Course Explorer are MATH241 and PHYS212. Familiarity with electric and magnetic fields, forces on charged particles, and basic calculus is helpful.

Prior plasma coursework is not required. The course begins with an electromagnetism review before introducing plasma concepts.

## When to Take It

NPRE321 is worth considering if you are completing the Semiconductor Engineering minor and want to understand plasmas used in semiconductor manufacturing. It provides background for processes such as thin-film deposition and etching, while the practicums connect lecture concepts to actual equipment.

ECE students can take the course after completing the prerequisites without waiting for upper-level ECE coursework. Students interested in continuing into plasma physics should also consider taking it before NPRE421, which requires NPRE321.

## Course Structure

The course normally meets for three 50-minute lectures each week. Four practicum units are spread throughout the semester, with each unit scheduled across two class periods in the Center for Plasma-Material Interactions laboratory.

The four practicums cover:

- Basic discharges and DC glow plasmas
- Plasma deposition and processing
- Atmospheric plasmas
- Fusion plasmas using the Hybrid Illinois Device for Research and Applications (HIDRA)

The Fall 2026 grading breakdown is:

- Homework: 56%, consisting of 14 assignments worth 4% each
- Test I: 20%
- Test II: 20%
- Practicum participation: 4%, with each practicum worth 1%

There are two tests: a midterm and an end-of-semester test. Both are scheduled for 120 minutes. The syllabus assigns the first test to the earlier lecture material and the second to the later material. Exams closely resemble the practice exams provided by Professor Andruczyk, so working through those problems is useful preparation.

Homework can be challenging because some questions leave the expected explanation or approach unclear. Homework following each practicum is a lab report about the demonstration. These reports are part of the homework grade, in addition to the separate practicum participation points.

Assignments are due online before class on the assigned due date. The Fall 2026 syllabus specifies a late penalty of 10% per day, capped at 50%, with late work accepted through the last week of class. Check the current syllabus for any policy changes.

## Instructors

Professor Daniel Andruczyk developed and teaches NPRE321. Professor Andruczyk conducts research on fusion plasmas and plasma-material interactions, including liquid lithium as a plasma-facing material, and leads research using HIDRA. The course incorporates laboratory demonstrations that connect plasma fundamentals to processing equipment and fusion research.

## Course Tips

Start homework early enough to ask questions about unclear instructions. Check what each problem expects you to calculate, explain, or draw, and include the reasoning behind your answer.

During practicums, listen carefully to the professor and lab coordinator. Take detailed notes and pictures of the apparatus when permitted. Record the purpose of the experiment, how the plasma is generated, what each component does, and what is being measured or demonstrated.

Most practicum reports require a diagram of the apparatus. Sketch and label the setup while it is in front of you, and make sure you understand how the components work together. Pictures are helpful, but your notes should also capture explanations given during the demonstration.

For exams, work through the provided practice exam and understand the reasoning behind each solution. Keep track of units, especially when working with electronvolts, kelvin, plasma densities, and characteristic frequencies. For particle-motion problems, draw the field and velocity directions before calculating forces or drifts.

## Life After

For ECE students interested in semiconductor fabrication, NPRE321 provides background for understanding plasma processing, deposition, etching, and interactions between plasmas and surfaces. These concepts connect with fabrication topics encountered in courses such as ECE444 (IC Device Theory and Fabrication).

Students interested in further plasma coursework can take NPRE421 (Plasma and Fusion Science), which develops particle and fluid descriptions, waves, collisions, stability, and confinement in greater depth. Other options include NPRE429 (Plasma Engineering) and NPRE423 (Plasma Laboratory), subject to their respective prerequisites.
