# ECE364

ECE364 (Programming Methods for Machine Learning) is a 3-credit-hour course that satisfies the Technical Elective requirement for ECE majors. This course also satisfies a lab requirement for EE majors.

## Content Covered

This course is an introduction to machine learning algorithms. The course begins with intoducing PyTorch and reviewing basic linear algebra/matrix calculus. The main contents of the course include automatic differentiation, linear/logistic regression, optimizers and data loaders, deep nets, transformers, self-supervised learning, and generative models. Students will complete a final project at the end of the course as a kaggle competition. They will chose from a selection of 2-3 topics and build a model to complete the task. In the spring 2026 semester, students could chose from computer vision, sentiment analysis, or prediction.

## Prerequisites

- [MATH257](../CS%20Course%20Offerings/MATH257.md)

## When to Take It

If you are interested in basic machine learning this class can be taken at any time, in accordance with the 2.25 rule. Students may want to take this class in a semester that they are taking heavier classes, as the homework and concepts are lighter compared to other 300/400-level ECE classes. Students may spend 3-5 hours per week on assignments in the class.

## Course Structure

- 25% Homework
- 25% Midterm 1
- 25% Midterm 2
- 25% Final Project

This clas is offered on Tuesdays/Thursdays, with the time varying by semester. There are 6 homework assignments in the class that will be submitted to gradescope. The two midterm assingments will be taken in class with a provided cheat sheet. The final project is about a month long and the grade is mostly based off of the 2-page lab report.

## Course Tips

As the lecture slides are Jupyter notebooks, you should download and use them out during/after lectures to apply the concepts taught. 

## Life After

If you enjoyed the the basics of machine learning and want to know the math in more detail, you may consider [ECE449](ECE449.md) for a more proof based approach to machine learning. If you are interested in surveying more algorithms, [ECE448](ECE448.md) covers applications and additional algorithms, with some overlap. Similarly, ECE494 may be interesting if you liked the hands on part of 364.
