# ECE333

ECE333 (Green Electric Energy) is a 3 credit hour course that counts as a technical elective for both EEs and CEs.  It is offered in the fall and spring semesters.

## Content Covered

- History of the electric power industry, energy policy
- Electric power grid
- Single phase power circuit analysis
- Wind energy conversion
- Economics
- Energy from the Sun
- Solar cell modeling
  
ECE333 begins with a brief overview of the history of the American electric power industry, including past and present energy policy. The course discusses the components of the electric grid, how different generation technologies supply electricity, and what goes into maintaining grid stability. Power circuit analysis covers RMS values, phasors, real, reactive, and complex power, power factor, power factor correction, and three-phase power.

The course then moves into wind energy conversion. Students learn about different types of wind turbines, rotors, and generators, and how altitude and wind shear affect the power available in the wind. Topics include the Betz limit, tip-speed ratio, the idealized turbine power curve, and wind-speed distributions. Students use the power curve and wind-speed distributions to estimate average power and energy production. The economics unit covers discount rates, the present value of recurring costs, and life-cycle cost comparisons. Earlier offerings have also covered cash flow, net present value, internal rate of return, and inflation.
The solar energy unit covers blackbody radiation, Planck’s law, the solar spectrum, and solar irradiation. Students learn how solar declination, altitude, and azimuth describe the Sun’s position, use sun-path diagrams to analyze shading, and estimate clear-sky direct-beam radiation. The course introduces basic semiconductor physics and p–n junctions, then covers solar cell current–voltage characteristics, equivalent circuit models, maximum power point, and fill factor. Students learn how cells are connected into modules and arrays, how to model a solar module, and how temperature, sunlight, and partial shading affect its output. The unit also covers bypass diodes, blocking diodes, and maximum power point tracking. The Spring 2026 offering included electric vehicles and their implications.

## Prerequisites

The prerequisite for ECE333 is ECE205 or [ECE210](ECE210.md). Familiarity with complex numbers and phasors from ECE210 is important for ECE333.

You can take ECE333 before [ECE330](ECE330.md). The first few lectures cover the power circuit fundamentals used in the course, including RMS values, phasors, complex power, power factor, and three-phase power.

## When to Take It

Take this course if you have an interest in renewable energy.  No courses directly follow ECE333, so there is no rush to take this course, unless you are specifically looking for internships in the renewable energy industry.  For students interested in the power and energy field, taking this course later may leave you with a greater appreciation of the concepts.  That being said, ECE333 can really be taken anytime after taking ECE 205 or [ECE210](ECE210.md).

## Course Structure

Weekly homework is split into two submissions. The first is graded on good-faith effort. After solutions are posted, students complete a second submission by reflecting on their work or rewriting their answers using the posted solutions. The second submission is required for every problem, even if the original answer was correct.

The course has two midterms, a final exam, and an individual project due near the end of the semester. For the first midterm, students are allowed to bring one cheat sheet, for the second midterm, students are allowed to bring two cheat sheets, and for the final, students are allowed to bring three cheat sheets.  It is highly recommended to make one new cheat sheet for each exam, as this will help greatly with studying.  Students on average usually do well on the exams in ECE333. ECE333 is graded on an absolute scale, so doing well on exams is important for doing well in the class. An optional virtual reality (VR) tour is available for extra credit.

## Course Tips

Do not skip lecture, as this will only require you to spend more time searching through the textbook in order to complete the homework.  While preparing for exams, go through your notes and through relevant sections of the textbook and be sure to write down unfamiliar concepts and formulas on your cheat sheet.

## Life After

ECE333 is an excellent course for students looking to work in the renewable energy industry, as much of what you learn in this course has direct applications to the industry.  No courses directly follow ECE333.  Students looking to pursue renewable energy further should look toward courses in the power field, beginning with [ECE330 - Power Circuits and Electromechanics](ECE330.md), if they have not already taken it.  After ECE330, students could consider [ECE431 - Electric Machinery](ECE431.md), [ECE464 - Power Electronics](ECE464.md), [ECE469 - Power Electronics Laboratory](ECE469.md), [ECE476 - Power System Analysis](ECE476.md), and [ECE486 - Control Systems](ECE486.md).  Students who enjoyed the section on solar energy conversion could also consider [ECE443 - LEDs and Solar Cells](ECE443.md).
