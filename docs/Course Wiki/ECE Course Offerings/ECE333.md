# ECE333

ECE333 (Green Electric Energy) is a 3 credit hour course that counts as a technical elective for both EEs and CEs.  It is offered in the fall and spring semesters.

## Content Covered

- History of the electric power industry, energy policy
- Electric power grid
- Single phase power circuit analysis
- Wind energy conversion
- Economics
- Energy from the Sun
- Solar cell modeling
  
ECE333 begins with the history of the electric power industry, how the grid is organized, and how different generation technologies supply electricity. The course then reviews RMS values and phasors before covering complex power, power factor, and three-phase power.

The wind energy unit covers power available in the wind, wind generator types, the Betz limit, tip-speed ratio, and turbine power curves. Students use wind-speed distributions and turbine power curves to estimate average power and energy production. The engineering economics unit covers discounting, the present value of recurring expenses, and how to compare energy projects using life-cycle cost.

The solar energy unit covers the solar spectrum, the Sun’s position, and clear-sky direct-beam radiation. Students use sun-path diagrams to determine when an obstacle shades a site. The course then introduces p–n junctions and solar cell modeling before moving from individual cells to photovoltaic modules and arrays. Students learn how temperature, sunlight, and partial shading affect current–voltage curves, and how bypass and blocking diodes and maximum power point tracking are used in PV systems.

The course wraps up with PV system design considerations and current issues in solar energy. Students also complete an individual project, and an optional virtual reality (VR) tour is available for extra credit.

## Prerequisites

The prerequisite for ECE333 is ECE205 or [ECE210](ECE210.md). Familiarity with complex numbers and phasors is helpful.

You can take ECE333 before [ECE330](ECE330.md). The first few lectures cover the power circuit fundamentals used in the course, including RMS values, phasors, complex power, power factor, and three-phase power.

## When to Take It

Take this course if you have an interest in renewable energy.  No courses directly follow ECE333, so there is no rush to take this course, unless you are specifically looking for internships in the renewable energy industry.  For students interested in the power and energy field, taking this course later may leave you with a greater appreciation of the concepts.  That being said, ECE333 can really be taken anytime after taking ECE 205 or [ECE210](ECE210.md).

## Course Structure

Weekly homework is split into two submissions. The first is graded on good-faith effort. After solutions are posted, students complete a second submission by reflecting on their work or rewriting their answers using the posted solutions. The second submission is required for every problem, even if the original answer was correct.

The course has two midterms, a final exam, and an individual project due near the end of the semester. An optional virtual reality (VR) tour is available for extra credit.

## Course Tips

Do not skip lecture, as this will only require you to spend more time searching through the textbook in order to complete the homework.  While preparing for exams, go through your notes and through relevant sections of the textbook and be sure to write down unfamiliar concepts and formulas on your cheat sheet.

## Life After

ECE333 is an excellent course for students looking to work in the renewable energy industry, as much of what you learn in this course has direct applications to the industry.  No courses directly follow ECE333.  Students looking to pursue renewable energy further should look toward courses in the power field, beginning with [ECE330 - Power Circuits and Electromechanics](ECE330.md), if they have not already taken it.  After ECE330, students could consider [ECE431 - Electric Machinery](ECE431.md), [ECE464 - Power Electronics](ECE464.md), [ECE469 - Power Electronics Laboratory](ECE469.md), [ECE476 - Power System Analysis](ECE476.md), and [ECE486 - Control Systems](ECE486.md).  Students who enjoyed the section on solar energy conversion could also consider [ECE443 - LEDs and Solar Cells](ECE443.md).
